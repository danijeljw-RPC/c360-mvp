Cinturon360 Mock MVP

A fully-simulated travel mid-office platform demonstrating booking finance, invoicing, payment orchestration, and audit — all without external service dependencies.

Mock Data Files Swagger / API

Introduction

Cinturon360 is a proposed travel technology platform designed for travel agencies, brokers, and Travel Management Companies (TMCs). This mockup is a local-only, fully deterministic demo system. No external APIs are called. No real payment data is processed. No credentials leave your machine.

The purpose of this demo is to prove one central architecture principle:

Core Principle:

Cinturon360 owns the internal booking-finance logic, ledger, invoice structure, and audit trail. Payment providers, finance systems, GDS/NDC imports, and BSP-style files are simulated channels only. The Cinturon360 internal ledger is always the source of truth.

The stack is: .NET 10ASP.NET CoreBlazor ServerEF CorePostgreSQL 17Docker Compose

Organisation Hierarchy & Architecture

The platform models a three-tier organisation hierarchy. Every billing, policy, and payment decision traces back through this hierarchy.

  • Platform Vendor
    Avanōa Technology
  • Travel Management Company (TMC)
    HelloWorld Travel
  • Client Organisation
    Coca-Cola

Responsibility Rules

  • Avanōa Technology operates the platform, executes rules, and routes payments. It is responsible only for platform operation unless explicitly the seller for a given relationship.
  • HelloWorld Travel onboards Coca-Cola. If HelloWorld configures billing for Coca-Cola, HelloWorld owns the credit and payment risk.
  • Coca-Cola is the client. Its travellers are billed according to travel policies and cost centres configured by HelloWorld.

Getting Started

The entire platform runs with a single command. No cloud account, no API keys, no external services required.

docker compose up -d

After the containers start (allow ~15 seconds for PostgreSQL to initialise and the API to seed data):

  1. Open your browser and navigate to the Web UI: http://localhost:5001
  2. You will be redirected to /login — use the seeded demo account below.
  3. After login you land on the Dashboard at /.
  4. Navigate using the sidebar to explore all modules.
  5. To explore the REST API interactively, go to http://localhost:5000/swagger.
  6. To download raw mock data files, go to http://localhost:5001/mock-data.
Tip — Reset to clean state:

Run docker compose down -v && docker compose up -d to destroy the database volume and reseed from scratch. All imported bookings, generated invoices, and payment attempts will be reset to their original seeded state.

Billing Relationships /billing-relationships

Lists every seller–buyer pair in the system. Each relationship defines:

  • Seller — the organisation that raises invoices and holds credit risk
  • Buyer — the organisation receiving the service or platform charge
  • Billing Mode — e.g. CardCharge, ManualInvoice, ExternalFinanceSystem, ShadowBilling
  • Payment Provider — which simulated provider is used for card collection
  • Payment Terms — days until balance is due
  • Currency — AUD for all demo relationships
  • Risk Held By Seller — confirms commercial risk ownership
The billing relationship record is what determines how an invoice is collected. When a booking is processed, the system looks up the relationship between the TMC (HelloWorld) and the client (Coca-Cola) to decide the billing mode and provider.

Lists all travel policies configured for Coca-Cola by HelloWorld Travel. Each policy has:

  • Billing Behaviour — controls how payment is routed: UseDefaultOrgMethod, UseSpecificCard, ManualApprovalRequired, ShadowBillingOnly
  • Payment Method — if the policy specifies a card, the display label of that card (e.g. APAC Mastercard, EMEA Amex)
Scenario 4 — Policy Payment Override: The APAC policy routes to the APAC Mastercard, EMEA routes to the EMEA Amex, and North America uses the default organisation billing. This is configured in the travel policy table and automatically applied during invoice generation.

Invoices List /invoices

Click View to open the Invoice Detail page for full line-item, payment, and action controls.

Invoice Detail /invoices/{id}

Displays the complete invoice including line items, payment schedule, and all recorded payment attempts. Actions available depend on the invoice's billing mode and current status:

Simulation Note: A banner on every invoice detail page reads: "Provider action simulated only. No external payment was made. Cinturon360 internal invoice remains the source of truth."

Simulate Collection

For card-based invoices. Simulates sending the invoice to the configured provider (Stripe, Airwallex, etc.). Returns either a Succeeded or Failed result deterministically based on the scenario.

Retry Payment

Available after a failed payment attempt. Creates a new PaymentAttempt record and re-simulates the provider call. Useful for demonstrating Scenario 5.

Mark Manual Payment Received

For ManualInvoice billing mode. Records that the client has paid outside the platform (bank transfer, cheque, etc.). Updates status to Paid and creates a ledger credit entry.

Mark Externally Settled

For invoices managed by an external finance system (Xero, NetSuite). Marks the invoice as ExternallyManaged so it is excluded from further internal collection actions.

The invoice detail page also shows the full Invoice Lines table (fare, taxes, service fees, adjustments) and the Payment Attempts history with timestamps, amounts, provider responses, and fake external reference IDs.

Internal Ledger /ledger

The ledger is the financial backbone of the platform. Every financial movement — invoice generation, payment receipt, failed attempt, manual settlement, ADM charge, ACM credit — creates a LedgerEntry.

The Cinturon360 internal ledger is the source of truth for all financial movements. Provider references (Stripe charge IDs, fake external refs) are recorded for traceability only — the ledger itself is owned by Cinturon360 and is not derived from provider data.

Columns: timestamp, entry type, description, debit amount, credit amount, currency, external reference. Entry types include: InvoiceGenerated, PaymentReceived, PaymentFailed, ManualSettlement, AdmCharge, AcmCredit, ExternalSettlement.

ADM/ACM Workbench /agency-memos

Agency Debit Memos (ADMs) and Agency Credit Memos (ACMs) are BSP-issued adjustments from airlines to agencies. This workbench handles their import, matching, and decisioning.

Importing

Click Import BSP CSV and paste CSV content in the format:

memo_number,memo_type,ticket_number,pnr,airline,reason_code,reason,amount,currency,status

A sample file is available at /mock-data/bsp/bsp-adm-acm-sample.csv

Decisioning

Each imported memo can be actioned from the list or its detail page:

Dispute

Marks the memo as disputed. In a live system this would initiate a dispute with the airline. Here it transitions the memo to Disputed status and logs the event.

Absorb Internally

The agency (HelloWorld) absorbs the cost. Creates a LedgerEntry debit against the agency. The client (Coca-Cola) is not recharged.

Recharge Client

The cost is passed on to Coca-Cola. Generates an adjustment invoice line on the original booking invoice and creates a ledger entry.

The memo detail page (/agency-memos/{id}) shows the matched booking, ticket, supplier, reason code, and complete action history.

Outstanding Balances

All unpaid invoices with booking reference, traveller, client, amount due, and due date.

ADM/ACM Exposure

Audit Log /audit

Every significant action in the platform writes an AuditEvent record. The audit log displays a chronological table of all events, including:

  • Timestamp — UTC datetime of the event
  • Actor — the email of the user who triggered the action
  • Entity Type — what kind of record was affected (Booking, Invoice, PaymentAttempt, AdmAcmMemo, etc.)
  • Action — what happened (Imported, InvoiceGenerated, PaymentSucceeded, PaymentFailed, ManualPaymentReceived, CostCentreAssigned, AdmDisputed, etc.)
  • Summary — human-readable description

The audit log is append-only and cannot be edited from the UI. It provides a complete, tamper-evident trace of all platform activity during the demo.

Simulated Payment Providers

All payment provider behaviour in Cinturon360 is deterministic simulation. No external API calls are made. Each provider name appears in billing relationships, travel policies, and invoice records purely as a routing label. The actual "collection" is a local function that returns a success or failure result based on the scenario configuration.

Supported provider types:

Stripe Airwallex Braintree PayPal Square Manual Invoice External Finance System Partner Managed Billing Shadow Billing / Trial

What "simulation" means in practice

When simulate-collection is called on an invoice, the API:

  1. Looks up the invoice's billing mode and provider type
  2. Checks the booking's scenario type to determine if the result should be Succeeded or Failed
  3. Creates a PaymentAttempt record with a fake external reference (e.g. ch_fake_stripe_...)
  4. Creates a corresponding LedgerEntry (debit for failed, credit for succeeded)
  5. Updates the invoice status accordingly
  6. Writes an AuditEvent
Why this matters for the demo: Even though the provider is simulated, the internal records — ledger, invoice, audit trail — are fully real database records with referential integrity. This proves that the Cinturon360 layer is provider-agnostic: swapping Stripe for Airwallex changes only the routing label and the API credential, not any of the core billing logic.

Ownership Principle — Cinturon360 Owns the Billing Logic

"Cinturon360 owns the internal booking-finance logic, ledger, invoice structure, and audit trail. Payment providers, finance systems, GDS/NDC imports, and BSP-style files are simulated channels only."

This principle is demonstrated concretely in the following ways throughout the UI and API:

Ledger as Source of Truth

Every financial action — regardless of which provider handles it — creates a LedgerEntry in the Cinturon360 database. The invoice detail page carries a persistent notice stating this explicitly. Provider references are stored for traceability, but the ledger is not derived from provider data.

Providers Are Channels

Stripe, Airwallex, and others appear as selectable routing targets. Changing the provider on a billing relationship changes where the collection is sent — it does not change the invoice structure, the ledger schema, or any booking finance calculation. The core logic is provider-neutral.

GDS / BSP Are Import Channels

PNR data from a GDS and BSP debit memo data are ingested as raw inputs. Once inside Cinturon360, they are normalised into the domain model (Booking, Segment, Document, AdmAcmMemo). The platform owns the validation, matching, and decisioning from that point forward.

Finance Systems Are Export Targets

Xero and NetSuite receive export files, but they do not define invoices. The invoice is generated, valued, and recorded in Cinturon360 first. The export is a secondary representation for external accounting. Marking an invoice ExternallyManaged does not delete or modify the internal record.

Policy Owns Routing Decisions

Travel policies determine which payment card is used and how billing flows. This is configured inside Cinturon360 — the client or provider cannot override it. The APAC card, EMEA card, and default org billing are all enforced at invoice generation time based on the booking's matched policy.

Audit Trail is Immutable

Every action — import, validation, invoice generation, payment attempt, ADM decision, export — is written to the AuditEvent table. The audit log cannot be edited from the UI. It provides a complete, chronological record of who did what, independent of external systems.

In summary: A travel agency using Cinturon360 could replace Stripe with Airwallex, replace Amadeus GDS with Sabre, or replace Xero with NetSuite — and none of those changes would affect the invoice numbers, the ledger, the audit trail, or the billing logic. The platform is the constant. The external systems are interchangeable.
